## The Problem, in Three Chapters

We begin with the problem—not because it is pleasant, but because it is the condition we must understand before we can do anything about it.

The three chapters that follow establish the case:

**Chapter 1 — The Information Deluge** describes the flood: the exponential growth of information, the inversion of the bottleneck from access to attention, and the costs we pay for drowning in it.

**Chapter 2 — Information Is Not Knowledge** draws the distinction that everything else depends on: information is external, transferable, and passive; knowledge is internal, integrated, and actionable. The confusion between them is the root of many of our problems—including the failures of the AI systems we build.

**Chapter 3 — The Knowledge Gap** explains why the gap between information and knowledge is widening, what it costs us, and why adding more information makes it worse rather than better.

By the end of this part, you should see the problem clearly. You should also see that it is not a problem of supply—not a lack of information, not a lack of technology, not a lack of intelligence. It is a problem of **method**.

The rest of the book is about that method.
