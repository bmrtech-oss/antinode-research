# A Note on Evidence

---

A book like this one mixes three kinds of claims, and you should know which is which.

## Established research

Much of what follows is not mine. The argument that attention is the scarce resource comes from **Herbert Simon**. The account of fast and slow thinking, and of heuristics and biases, comes from **Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky**. The distinction between what we can say and what we can use comes from **Michael Polanyi**. The idea that knowledge grows by surviving refutation comes from **Karl Popper**. The case that expertise is built by deliberate practice comes from **K. Anders Ericsson**, and the study of how experts decide under pressure comes from **Gary Klein**. The view of the brain as a prediction engine is set out most fully by **Andy Clark**. The claim that tools can become part of the mind comes from Clark and **David Chalmers**. The separation of information from meaning comes from **Claude Shannon**.

These are findings and arguments other people made, tested, and in several cases disputed. I have used them as I understand them, and the reader who wants the originals should go to them.

## My synthesis

The **seven-stage knowledge pipeline** is a synthesis. Each stage draws on existing work: selection on attention research, testing on Popper, correction on error-driven learning. The combination, the ordering, and the claim that it can be practiced as a discipline are mine. I have not tested it in a controlled study, and I do not claim it is the only way to organize the work. It is a working method, refined by use and by failure.

## My formulations

Some phrases in this book are my own, and I use them as names for ideas I found I needed: *recall is not selection*; *the test that cannot fail is a mirror*; *the fallacy in technical clothing*; *the courage tax*; *scaffold debt*; *confident gap-filling*; *temporal leakage*; *commensurable and incommensurable values*; *the confidence–stakes calibration*; *the wasted-day insight*. They come from my own engineering work. They are observations, not results, and they have not been independently validated. Some may already have names elsewhere that I do not know; if so, the earlier name deserves the credit.

## The stories

The opening story of each chapter is personal experience, told as I remember it. It illustrates an idea; it is not evidence for it.

## What this means for you

Where the book makes claims, they are mine to defend. Where it cites research, the citation is in Appendix D.
