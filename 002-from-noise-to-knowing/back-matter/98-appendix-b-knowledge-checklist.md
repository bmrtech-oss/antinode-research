# Appendix B — The Knowledge Extraction Checklist

---

## How to Use This Appendix

This is the **operational manual** for the pipeline. Chapter 15 introduced the checklists; this appendix consolidates them into a single field guide.

Three ways to use it:

1. **As a pre-flight check.** Before starting a serious inquiry, scan the Quick Reference to remind yourself what the process requires.
2. **As a stage-by-stage guide.** When you are working through a particular stage, turn to that stage's checklist and questions.
3. **As a post-flight audit.** When you have finished, use the Post-Flight Review to verify that you have not skipped anything important.

The checklists are designed to be **printed, annotated, and modified**. They are not sacred. Adapt them to your domain. Add questions. Remove what does not apply. The goal is a working tool, not a perfect form.

## Stage 1 — Selection

**Question:** *What deserves my attention?*

### Checklist

- [ ] **Purpose defined.** I can state the question I am trying to answer or the goal I am trying to achieve.
- [ ] **Sources identified.** I know where the relevant information is likely to be found.
- [ ] **Credibility assessed.** I have evaluated sources for expertise, track record, incentives, evidence, transparency, and independence.
- [ ] **Relevance checked.** The information bears on my purpose—not just on something interesting.
- [ ] **Novelty assessed.** The information tells me something I do not already know, or changes my model.
- [ ] **Ignorance examined.** I have identified what I am choosing not to attend to, and why.
- [ ] **Attention protected.** I have minimized distractions and created conditions for focus.

### Red Flags

- I am consuming information because it is available, not because it is relevant.
- I am reading sources that all agree with each other.
- I am avoiding a source because I suspect it will challenge me.
- I cannot state my question in one sentence.

### Stage Audit

> *If I had to defend my selection to a skeptical colleague, could I? Could I name the sources I rejected and explain why?*

---

## Stage 2 — Pre-processing

**Question:** *Is this information in a form I can use?*

### Checklist

- [ ] **Cleaned.** I have checked for errors, inconsistencies, ambiguities, omissions, and biases.
- [ ] **Structured.** I have organized the information into categories, hierarchies, sequences, matrices, or networks that serve my purpose.
- [ ] **Contextualized.** I have restored the semantic, situational, historical, causal, and purposive context.
- [ ] **Connected.** I have linked the information to what I already know—similarities, differences, causes, contradictions, implications.
- [ ] **Provenance recorded.** I know where the information came from, who produced it, and why.
- [ ] **Assumptions identified.** I know what must be true for this information to be valid.

### Red Flags

- I am accepting the information at face value.
- I am skipping the context because I am in a hurry.
- I cannot explain what this information means to someone else.
- I have not written anything down.

### Stage Audit

> *Could I reconstruct this information from my notes in six months? Could someone else?*

---

## Stage 3 — Generation

**Question:** *What might explain this? What could be true?*

### Checklist

- [ ] **Concepts defined.** The key terms are clear, precise, and consistently used.
- [ ] **Hypotheses formed.** I have proposed at least two candidate models or explanations.
- [ ] **Alternatives considered.** I have generated hypotheses that contradict my initial intuition.
- [ ] **Analogies explored.** I have drawn connections to other domains that might illuminate this one.
- [ ] **Synthesis attempted.** I have tried to combine the pieces into a coherent whole.
- [ ] **Assumptions surfaced.** I know what my hypotheses assume, and which assumptions are questionable.
- [ ] **Falsifiability checked.** Each hypothesis makes predictions that could be tested.

### Red Flags

- I have only one hypothesis, and it is the one I started with.
- My hypotheses are vague enough that no evidence could refute them.
- I am generating ideas but not recording them.
- I am confusing novelty with truth.

### Stage Audit

> *If I were wrong, what would I likely have missed? Have I generated the hypothesis that would explain that?*

---

## Stage 4 — Testing

**Question:** *Does this hold up against evidence?*

### Checklist

- [ ] **Predictions derived.** I know what each hypothesis predicts about what I would observe.
- [ ] **Evidence gathered.** I have compared predictions against observations, data, or experience.
- [ ] **Disconfirmation sought.** I have actively looked for evidence that would prove my hypothesis wrong.
- [ ] **Rival hypotheses compared.** I have identified evidence that discriminates between competing explanations.
- [ ] **Limits acknowledged.** I know what my evidence does not establish.
- [ ] **Confounds considered.** I have identified third variables that could explain the relationship.
- [ ] **Base rates checked.** I have considered the prior probability, not just the specific evidence.

### Red Flags

- I am only looking for evidence that supports my hypothesis.
- I am dismissing disconfirming evidence as an exception.
- I am confusing correlation with causation.
- I am more confident than the evidence warrants.

### Stage Audit

> *What is the strongest piece of evidence against my hypothesis? Have I engaged with it honestly, or explained it away?*

---

## Stage 5 — Integration

**Question:** *How does this fit with what I already know?*

### Checklist

- [ ] **Coherent.** The new knowledge is consistent with my existing knowledge—or I have identified and resolved the contradiction.
- [ ] **Consilient.** The new knowledge connects to knowledge from other domains.
- [ ] **Hierarchical.** I know whether this is a foundational principle, a mid-level theory, or a specific fact.
- [ ] **Applicable.** I know what the new knowledge implies for action.
- [ ] **Connected.** I have linked the new knowledge to related concepts and models.
- [ ] **Contradictions resolved.** I have either resolved conflicts with existing knowledge or acknowledged them explicitly.

### Red Flags

- The new knowledge sits in isolation, unconnected to anything else.
- I have not checked whether it contradicts something I already believe.
- I cannot say what difference this knowledge makes.
- I am integrating too eagerly—connecting things that are not actually related.

### Stage Audit

> *If I had to draw a map of my knowledge on this topic, where would this new piece go? What would it connect to? What would it replace?*

---

## Stage 6 — Retain or Discard

**Question:** *What is worth keeping?*

### Checklist

- [ ] **Predictive power.** The knowledge helps me anticipate what will happen.
- [ ] **Explanatory depth.** The knowledge helps me understand why things happen.
- [ ] **Coherence.** The knowledge fits with the rest of my knowledge base.
- [ ] **Actionability.** The knowledge guides my decisions and actions.
- [ ] **Relevance.** The knowledge matters for my goals and purposes.
- [ ] **Pruning done.** I have actively discarded what is obsolete, irrelevant, or disconfirmed.
- [ ] **Review scheduled.** I have a plan to revisit and reinforce what matters.

### Red Flags

- I am keeping everything because I cannot decide what to discard.
- I am keeping nothing because I am waiting for certainty.
- I have not reviewed my knowledge base in months.
- I cannot find what I need when I need it.

### Stage Audit

> *If my knowledge base were a physical space, would it be a library or a hoard? Could I navigate it? Would I want to spend time there?*

---

## Stage 7 — Correct

**Question:** *What was wrong, and what have I learned?*

### Checklist

- [ ] **Beliefs revised.** I have updated specific beliefs in light of new evidence.
- [ ] **Models revised.** I have revised the underlying frameworks that produced the beliefs.
- [ ] **Process improved.** I have improved the method by which I acquire knowledge.
- [ ] **Disconfirmation sought.** I have actively looked for evidence that could prove me wrong.
- [ ] **Error welcomed.** I have treated being wrong as an opportunity to become less wrong.
- [ ] **Prediction recorded.** I have written down what I expect, so I can check it later.
- [ ] **Humility practiced.** I have acknowledged the limits of my knowledge.

### Red Flags

- I cannot remember the last time I changed my mind about something important.
- I am defending my beliefs rather than testing them.
- I am avoiding people who disagree with me.
- I am more confident than I was a year ago, but I cannot say why.

### Stage Audit

> *What do I believe now that I did not believe a year ago? What do I believe a year ago that I no longer believe? If the answer to either is "nothing," I am not correcting.*

## The Pre-Flight Check

Before starting any significant inquiry, run this.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      PRE-FLIGHT CHECK                               │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                     │
│  □ I can state my question in one sentence.                         │
│                                                                     │
│  □ I know what a good answer would look like.                       │
│                                                                     │
│  □ I know what would prove me wrong.                                │
│                                                                     │
│  □ I have identified at least three credible sources.               │
│                                                                     │
│  □ I have identified at least one source that will challenge me.    │
│                                                                     │
│  □ I have set aside time for deep work.                             │
│                                                                     │
│  □ I have a place to record what I find.                            │
│                                                                     │
│  □ I am willing to change my mind.                                  │
│                                                                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

<!-- diagram-alt -->*Diagram description: A pre-flight checklist presents eight questions to answer before research, covering the question, desired answer, disconfirming evidence, credible and challenging sources, time, note-taking, and openness to changing one?s mind.*


If you cannot check all of these, you are not ready to begin. Fix what is missing first.

## The Reading Protocol

When reading a book, paper, or long article, run this protocol.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      READING PROTOCOL                               │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│                                                                     │
│  BEFORE READING:                                                    │
│  □ What is my question?                                             │
│  □ Why am I reading this?                                           │
│  □ What do I already know about this topic?                         │
│  □ What do I expect to find?                                        │
│                                                                     │
│  DURING READING:                                                    │
│  □ What is the author's main claim?                                 │
│  □ What evidence is offered?                                        │
│  □ What assumptions are being made?                                 │
│  □ What is being left out?                                          │
│  □ What do I disagree with?                                         │
│  □ What surprises me?                                               │
│                                                                     │
│  AFTER READING:                                                     │
│  □ Can I summarize the argument in three sentences?                 │
│  □ What did I learn that I did not know before?                     │
│  □ What did I learn that changes my model?                          │
│  □ What do I need to verify or follow up on?                        │
│  □ What should I discard?                                           │
│                                                                     │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

<!-- diagram-alt -->*Diagram description: A reading protocol organizes questions into before, during, and after reading, moving from purpose and prior knowledge to claims, evidence, assumptions, and omissions, then to summary, model changes, verification, and discarding.*

## A Final Word

The checklist is not the knowledge. The checklist is the **scaffold**—a temporary structure that supports the process until it becomes a habit. Once the habit is built, you will not need the checklist. You will ask the questions automatically. You will run the stages without thinking about them.

But until then, use the checklist. Print it. Annotate it. Modify it. Make it yours.

The goal is not to follow the checklist perfectly. The goal is to **internalize the discipline**—to make the pipeline a part of how you think, so that you can apply it flexibly, adaptively, and efficiently.

The checklist is a means. The discipline is the end.
