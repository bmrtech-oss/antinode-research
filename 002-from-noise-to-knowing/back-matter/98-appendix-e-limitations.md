# Appendix E — Limitations

---

A method that does not say where it stops working is not finished. These are the places this one stops, as far as I can see them today.

## It does not solve motivation

The pipeline assumes you want to know. It gives you stages, checklists, and practices, and none of them supplies the wish to use them. Chapter 15 describes a system I built and abandoned after eleven days. The lesson there was about weight: I tried to install everything at once. But behind the weight is a plainer fact. Any practice has to be started again each morning, and nothing in this book can do that for you. A discipline can lower the cost of effort. It cannot create the reason for the effort.

If you do not have a question that matters to you, the pipeline has nothing to work on. Every exercise in Chapter 15 begins with a question you care about, and that is not an accident. It is the one input I cannot provide.

## It does not solve institutional power

Chapter 18 describes the courage tax: the cost of dissent is paid by the dissenter, and the benefit is shared. I offer countermeasures, such as rotating a devil's advocate and protecting dissenters. They work only where someone with authority chooses to adopt them. A junior engineer cannot install them. Where those with power are not interested in being corrected, individual discipline will produce better private beliefs and little change in what the organization does. I have not found a method that fixes that, and I do not claim this one does.

## It is individual-first

The book treats knowing as something a mind does, and then extends the argument to teams. It does not address collective knowledge production at scale: how a scientific field, a newsroom, or a public sphere comes to know something, and how it fails to. Those problems have their own literatures and their own mechanisms. An individual who practices the pipeline well may still live inside a system that produces confident error. The book is a guide to your own reasoning, not to the epistemic health of a society.

## It assumes a baseline many readers lack

The practices assume time to read and reflect, enough literacy and numeracy to evaluate evidence, access to sources worth selecting among, and enough safety to dissent, to be wrong in public, and to revise. Not everyone has these. A person working two jobs has little time for a prediction journal. A person whose dissent carries real danger cannot treat the courage tax as a design variable, because for them it is not a tax but a threat.

I wrote this book from inside a technical profession in which questioning a conclusion is usually survivable. That shapes what I notice and what I recommend. If your circumstances are different, some of this will be advice you cannot afford to follow, and that is a limit of the book, not a failing of yours.

## Its claims about AI are time-bound

Chapters 21 through 23 describe context engineering, working with language models, and data science as they stand as I write. The specific techniques will be superseded. Models will get better at some of the failures I describe and worse at others, and new failures will appear. Tool names, interface conventions, and the particular fixes I recommend should be read as a snapshot, and you should check them against current practice before relying on them.

I expect the structure of the argument to hold longer than the examples: that fluency is not reliability, that extension is not substitution, that a test must be able to fail. But that is a prediction, and I have been wrong about the shelf life of technical ideas before.

## It does not decide what you should value

The pipeline can tell you whether a claim survived testing. It cannot tell you what to do with a result when your values pull in different directions. Chapter 25 argues that some values are incommensurable, and that no method removes the need to weigh them. The pipeline can make the weighing better informed. It cannot do the weighing, and it would be a mistake to read a well-tested conclusion as a settled decision.

## It is a method, not a proof

Finally, the pipeline is a synthesis and a working method, as the note on evidence says. I have used it and it has helped me. That is not the same as having shown that it works for others, or better than alternatives. If you try it and it fails, I would rather know than not. A method offered in good faith should be easy to criticize, and the places where this one breaks are the places where it can be improved.
