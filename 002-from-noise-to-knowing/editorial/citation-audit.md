The UNVERIFIED count is high because most sentences in the book state general knowledge without a source. UNVERIFIED does not mean wrong. It means the book gives no source for it that the audit can confirm. The audit did not rewrite these sentences, because softening about 190 of them would change the voice. They are listed below for the author to decide, one by one.

## What was changed in the text

- 22 citations were added in chapters where Appendix D supports the claim (mostly Kahneman and Tversky in Chapters 1, 3, 7 and 16; Janis, Clark and Chalmers, Luhmann, Ahrens and Forte in Chapters 18?21; Popper, Clark in Chapters 6 and 11).
- Nothing else in the chapters was reworded by the audit.
- Appendix C: seven textbook-style examples were replaced with examples from the book's own domain (equivocation, amphiboly, accent, begging the question, false dilemma, ignoring a common cause, appeal to emotion). The brief's "three tails" and "beating your mother" examples are not in Appendix C. The original "feather" example was replaced.
- Finis: "Karl Feynman" corrected to "Richard Feynman", which matches the Part III, IV and VI dividers.
- Part V divider and table-of-contents epigraph: the Vannevar Bush line now carries the full source ("As We May Think," *The Atlantic Monthly*, July 1945).

## Vannevar Bush quote: recommendation

**Keep.** It is one sentence from a 1945 essay and is quoted as an epigraph, which is within ordinary fair use. The source is now named, and "As We May Think" is in Appendix D. The quote is on the Part V divider and in the table of contents. It is not in Chapter 19, which cites Clark and Chalmers instead. Before print, the author should check the wording against the published text, because it was not re-checked against the original here.

## Recommended edits not applied

These rewrite the author's sentence, so they are for the author to accept or reject. Several would soften a claim that the text states firmly. Two need particular attention:

- Chapter 24 says research on deliberate practice is "remarkably consistent: about ten years". The audit judges that this overstates the evidence. It suggests saying that research emphasises deliberate practice and does not establish a universal ten-year requirement.
- Chapter 6 says the older model of the brain "has been overturned" by predictive processing. The audit suggests "an influential account" instead.
